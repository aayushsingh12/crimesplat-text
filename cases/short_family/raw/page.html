Short Family Case File
Jennifer Renee Short, Michael Short and Mary Short

Victims: Michael Wayne Short, 50; Mary Hall Short, 36; Jennifer Renee Short, 9

Location: Oak Level/Bassett area, Henry County, Virginia

Michael and Mary Discovered: August 15, 2002

Jennifer’s Remains Discovered: September 25, 2002, Rockingham County, North Carolina

Case: Triple homicide and child abduction

Virginia State Police Case Number: 02-19828

Status: Unsolved, active homicide investigation

Short Family Murder and Abduction Timeline

Who killed Short Family?

February 18, 1952

  • Michael Wayne Short was born. He grew up with three siblings, one brother and two sisters.
  • Michael Short did not finish high school.
  • He married his first wife at age 17.
  • Michael went through three divorces.
  • Michael’s uncle, Frank Arrington, described Michael as having a rough-and-tumble childhood and said Michael was not pampered growing up.

April 20, 1966

  • Mary Frances Hall was born.
  • Mary Hall grew up on a tobacco farm in Franklin County, Virginia.
  • According to her brother Carloyn Hodges, Mary was the most outgoing of the family’s three children, although she was generally quiet around people she did not know.
  • Hodges said Mary was “rarely outgoing” around strangers but enjoyed talking and spending time with people she knew.

1985 – 1990

  • By the mid-1980s, Michael had met Mary Hall in Rocky Mount, Virginia, at a bar called The Blue Jeans.
  • Michael and Mary met and began a relationship. The couple later married and settled in Henry County, Virginia.
  • 1990s: Michael drove a truck for Rocky Mount Ready Mix for about five years. Source
    • Ronnie Ray described Michael as a good worker.
    • Ray said that after Jennifer was born, Michael often talked about her proudly.

1992

  • Mary Short worked at the Pluma Inc. plant in the Bowles Industrial Park. Coworkers reportedly knew her as “Little Mary.”
  • During this period, Mary reportedly experienced unwanted attention from an unidentified man who came to the plant looking for her on multiple occasions.
  • In one reported 1992 incident, the man entered the plant and a manager ordered him to leave. He reportedly left in a white pickup truck.
  • The encounters later became important to investigators following Mary’s murder. In August 2002, police received information about a possible former stalker, and investigators released older photographs of Mary while trying to identify the man.

July 12, 1993

  • Michael and Mary welcomed their daughter, Jennifer Renee Short.
  • Jennifer was Michael’s fourth child, and the only child Michael and Mary had together.

January 1997

  • M.S. Mobile Home Movers Inc. Established: Michael operated a mobile-home moving business known as M.S. Mobile Home Movers Inc. Source
  • MS Mobile Home Movers Inc. held a license #2705040949 with the Virginia Contractors License Board, expiring on 08-31-2003. Source

December 2001

  • Family Friend Visits the Shorts: Family friend Sheila Davis stopped by the Short residence to drop off payment for work Michael had completed. Davis recalled that what was intended to be a brief 15-minute visit turned into approximately an hour of conversation with the family. Source
  • Davis later described Michael and Mary as hardworking, close to Jennifer and devoted to spending time together as a family.
  • Mary regularly helped Michael with his mobile-home moving work, including unloading cinder blocks.
  • Michael and Mary rarely used a babysitter because, according to Davis, they preferred spending their free time with Jennifer.
  • Short Family Planned Move to Callaway: Michael and Mary Short were planning to move back to Callaway, Virginia, where Mary had grown up. Source

Spring and Summer 2002

  • Michael and Mary Short operated M.S. Mobile Home Movers, a business that moved mobile homes.
  • The Short family was considering relocating to South Carolina. As part of that possibility, Michael traveled to areas of South Carolina seeking employment.
  • Investigators later specifically identified the areas around:
    • Bennettsville, South Carolina
    • Florence, South Carolina
    • Conway, South Carolina
    • Myrtle Beach, South Carolina
  • Those trips would become important enough that investigators traveled to South Carolina eight years after the murders to locate and interview people who may have interacted with Michael there. Source

Summer 2002

  • Jennifer completed third grade.
  • She spent considerable time with her parents and frequently accompanied Michael on nonschool days while he worked.
  • Jennifer turned 9 on July 12, 2002.
  • Her parents bought school supplies and children’s clothing to prepare for the upcoming school year.

July 12, 2002

  • Jennifer celebrated her 9th birthday.

August 1, 2002

  • Jennifer was enrolled to return to Figsboro Elementary School for fourth grade.
  • Michael continued making trips to South Carolina while the family considered relocating.
    • Investigators have subsequently identified this two-week period as particularly important. Source
    • Authorities continue to seek anyone who had contact with Michael, Mary or Jennifer Short between August 1 and August 14, 2002.
    • They also want information from anyone whose mobile home Michael’s company moved during 2002 and who investigators have never interviewed.

August 5, 2002

  • Michael and Mary took a concrete step toward leaving their Henry County home.
  • The Shorts contacted real estate agent Marlene Dalton and asked her to put their house up for sale.
  • Michael reportedly explained that his business was slow and that the family had a mobile home where they could live temporarily.
  • An open house was scheduled for Sunday, August 18, 2002, three days after Michael and Mary were found murdered and Jennifer disappeared. Source

August 12, 2002

  • Jennifer Seen at Concrete Business: Former Short employee Ronnie Ray, owner of Rocky Mount Ready Mix, said he last saw Jennifer when Michael brought her with him to Ray’s concrete business on August 12, three days before the murders. Source
    • Ray remembered Jennifer proudly showing him her third-grade report card and said she was “smiling and grinning.”
    • Michael was already talking about Jennifer beginning school soon and how well she had done in third grade.

August 13, 2002

  • Garry Storm Bowman, a carpenter who had lived in the Mayodan, North Carolina area, allegedly asked his landlord Rick Lemons for help moving his mobile home. Source
    • According to Lemons, Bowman appeared angry and referred to a man he said owed him money.
    • Lemons later told investigators Bowman said words to the effect that “he would have to kill him.”
    • This conversation became significant after investigators learned Bowman left the area shortly after the Short family murders.
  • 11:13 p.m. – Circle C Convenience Store: Surveillance footage captured Jennifer Short inside the Circle C convenience store near her family’s home. The footage was recorded less than two days before Michael and Mary Short were discovered murdered, and Jennifer was reported missing. Source
  • The surveillance image provides one of the final known visual records of Jennifer before her disappearance.

August 14, 2002

  • Evening: Michael Short spent part of the evening with his employee, Chris Thompson, working on a vehicle at the Short property. Source
    • Thompson stayed at a motel near the Short residence and returned after leaving Michael that evening.
    • Thompson told investigators that Michael, Mary and Jennifer were alive when he left the residence. Later accounts state that Jennifer had already gone to bed. Thompson would become one of the last known people to see the family before the murders.
    • Thompson and Michael had plans for the following morning. They were supposed to travel to Christiansburg, Virginia, to pick up a truck for Michael’s mobile-home moving business.
  • 10:51 p.m.: A Walmart receipt dated August 14, 2002, and timestamped 10:51 p.m. was recovered during the investigation of the Short residence. The receipt provides a documented transaction from the family’s final evening before Michael and Mary Short were murdered and Jennifer Short was abducted. Source
  • 11:05 p.m Michael, Mary, and Jennifer went through a Burger King drive-through in the Collinsville area late that night
    • The Short family spent what would become their final evening at their Oak Level home. Source
    • Investigators believe the murders and Jennifer’s abduction occurred sometime between midnight on August 14 and approximately 9:00 a.m. on August 15, 2002.

August 15, 2002

  • Between 12:00 a.m. and approximately 9:00 a.m.: Someone entered or gained access to the Short residence. Source
    • At some point during the overnight or early morning hours, the person or persons responsible entered or gained access to the Short residence.
    • An unidentified man was reportedly seen parked along U.S. Highway 220 near the Short home during the early morning hours. Investigators later sought to identify the man and the vehicle associated with the sighting.
    • The telephone line serving the Short residence was cut.
    • Michael Short was shot once in the head.
    • Mary Short was shot once in the head.
    • Investigators recovered two .22-caliber cartridge casings, one from Mary Short’s bed and another from the garage floor near Michael Short.
    • Jennifer Short disappeared from the residence during the same general period and was believed to have been abducted.
    • Authorities have never publicly established how long Jennifer remained alive after she was taken from the home.
  • 9:00 a.m. Michael Short’s employee, Chris Thompson, arrived at the Short residence to meet Michael. The two planned to travel to Christiansburg, Virginia, to pick up a truck for Michael’s mobile-home moving business.  Source
    • Thompson had worked for Michael for approximately six months.
    • He arrived in his pickup truck, honked the horn, and yelled, expecting Michael to come outside. Source
    • When no one responded, Thompson entered the house.
    • Michael had a couch in the garage and sometimes slept there.
    • Thompson found Michael lying on the couch. As he approached, he realized Michael was not sleeping and appeared to have suffered a gunshot wound to the head. Source
    • The Roanoke Times reported: An August 17, 2002 Roanoke Times report specifically described Michael as naked when Thompson discovered him on the garage couch. This detail is not consistently reported in other accounts of the discovery. Source
    • Thompson contacted authorities.
    • Thompson contacted authorities.
  • Henry County Sheriff’s Office deputies responded and confirmed that 50-year-old Michael Wayne Short was dead.
    • Wayne Davis, then a Henry County patrol deputy and now sheriff, was among the first officers to respond. Source
    • Deputies found Michael Short on a couch in the attached garage area of the residence. He had been shot once in the head. Source
    • Officers entered the residence. Mary Short was found dead in her bed. She also had been shot once in the head.
      • Later reporting concerning the crime scene described Mary’s mattress as having been displaced by approximately two inches and her pillow as being on the bedroom floor. Source
      • There was no indication in the publicly released evidence that Michael or Mary had engaged in an extended physical struggle with their killer.
    • Investigators described the shootings as execution-style killings. Source
  • Jennifer Is Missing: After finding Michael and Mary, officers discovered that their 9-year-old daughter, Jennifer Renee Short, was missing. Source
    • Jennifer was not found inside the residence. Officers did not immediately locate her elsewhere on the property.
    • The discovery created two investigations simultaneously:
      • The murders of Michael and Mary Short
      • The disappearance of Jennifer Short
      • Authorities initially considered whether Jennifer could have escaped or hidden during the shootings. After searches around the property failed to locate her, investigators increasingly believed she had been abducted by the person or persons responsible for killing her parents.
      • Officers and search teams scoured the area surrounding the Short home.
  • Crime Scene and Initial Search: Investigators secured the Short property
    • Michael Short had been shot once in the head. A .22-caliber shell casing was recovered near Michael.
    • Mary Short had been shot once in the head. A second .22-caliber shell casing was recovered near Mary.
    • The telephone line to the home had been cut.
    • Jennifer was missing.
    • Investigators began processing the residence for fingerprints, biological material, firearms evidence, and other trace evidence.
  • Investigators began an intensive search around the residence and the surrounding Henry County area. Investigators searched wooded terrain, roads, structures, and other areas where Jennifer might have gone if she had fled.
  • Search-and-rescue personnel and volunteers eventually became involved.
  • Law enforcement also began interviewing:
    • Relatives
    • Neighbors
    • Friends
    • Michael’s employees
    • Business associates
    • People familiar with the Short family
    • Registered sex offenders and other potential offenders
    • The investigation rapidly expanded beyond Henry County.
  • 4:25 p.m. An alert for Jennifer was distributed through the developing AMBER Alert system. Jennifer’s disappearance received widespread regional and eventually national attention.
  • Same Day: Bowman’s landlord, Rick Lemons, later reported that Bowman made a telephone call concerning killing a man in Virginia whom he said had failed to pay him for trailer work. Source
    • According to the October 5 report, Bowman had done odd jobs for Lemons and for Gary Lemons, Lemons’ landlord.
    • Bowman reportedly worked on mobile homes in the area and was described as a carpenter.

August 16, 2002

  • The physical search continued. Investigators worked to reconstruct the Shorts’ movements before the murders and determine who had recently interacted with the family. The working theory remained that Jennifer could still be alive.
  • Garrison Bowman Leaves North Carolina: Neighbors later told investigators that Garrison “Gary” Bowman, a 66-year-old carpenter from Rockingham County, North Carolina, left the area on August 16, the day after the Short family crime was discovered. Source
    • Bowman traveled north and ultimately entered Canada.
    • His departure would later receive intense scrutiny after Jennifer’s remains were discovered near property associated with him.
    • At this point, however, Bowman had not been publicly connected to the Short murders.
  • Landlord: Rick Lemons entered the trailer Bowman had rented from him. Source
    • Lemons said the trailer was essentially empty.
    • Police later searched the residence and found a map of Henry County with a red mark in the area of the Shorts’ home.
    • Investigators also found children’s clothing inside the residence.
    • Police seized bedclothes and hair samples from the trailer for examination.

August 17, 2002

  • Search activity continued. Jennifer’s photographs and home video were distributed publicly to generate sightings.
  • The investigation received national exposure. Authorities continued emphasizing that they hoped Jennifer remained alive.
  • Bowman’s landlord returned to the residence and spoke with authorities about Bowman. Source
    • According to the later investigative chronology, this helped investigators focus on Bowman.

August 18, 2002

  • Volunteer searchers continued examining the countryside.
  • Search dogs were used. Investigators continued receiving and evaluating tips. No confirmed trace of Jennifer was found.

August 19, 2002

  • Authorities continued expanding the geographical search. Investigators followed leads in surrounding jurisdictions. No suspect had been identified publicly.
  • Police filed results of the search of the Short home. 64 items were removed, including
    • Two .22-caliber shell casings found near Michael and Mary’s bodies and several weapons.
    • Police were searching for a red or dark-colored truck seen leaving the home on the morning of the murders.
    • Police believed the murderer knew the family.

August 20, 2002

  • Jennifer’s Belongings and Possible Winston-Salem Sightings: Investigators obtained another search warrant for the Short residence. Investigators sought items that could help establish what Jennifer had with her when she disappeared.
  • Among the property investigators were reportedly interested in were recently purchased school supplies and children’s clothing.
  • Determining whether specific clothing or belongings were missing could help establish what Jennifer may have been wearing or whether someone deliberately took possessions with her.
  • Two sightings in the Winston-Salem, North Carolina area received significant investigative attention.
  • One witness reported seeing a girl believed to resemble Jennifer in or around a dark-colored truck. The witness reported that the adult male associated with the vehicle displayed or pointed a firearm.
  • Another witness later reported seeing a girl resembling Jennifer with an adult male near a convenience store.
  • Law enforcement investigated both sightings. Neither sighting was ultimately confirmed as Jennifer.
  • Because Jennifer’s remains were later recovered in North Carolina, these reports remained notable but have never been publicly established as genuine sightings of her.

August 21, 2002

  • Family Pleads for Jennifer’s Return: Jennifer’s relatives continued to appeal publicly for her safe return. Ruby Young, Mary Short’s sister, and Ruby’s husband, Chris Young, a deputy with the Henry County Sheriff’s Office, appeared with other members of the Short family and asked whoever had Jennifer to release her. Source
    • Ruby Young read a statement on behalf of the family pleading directly with the person or persons responsible: “Please release her so she can come home.”
    • The family said they missed and loved Jennifer and asked that she not be harmed and be returned safely.
    • The family also urged anyone with information about Jennifer’s location to contact authorities.
    • The appearance marked the first time members of the Short family had spoken publicly since Michael and Mary were found murdered and Jennifer disappeared on August 15.
    • Ruby and Chris Young were joined by other relatives, including Carol Whitehead, another sister of Mary Short, and her husband James Whitehead.
    • Henry County Sheriff Frank Cassell said investigators had reported no breaks in either Jennifer’s abduction or the murders of Michael and Mary.
    • Cassell said investigators were continuing to pursue leads, most of them in or near Henry County.
  • Authorities acknowledged that investigators were examining several individuals. No one was formally identified as Jennifer’s abductor or the killer of her parents.
  • A visitation was held for Michael and Mary Short. Jennifer remained missing.

August 22, 2002

  • Funeral services were held for Michael and Mary.
  • Their daughter had not been located. The investigation continued as the family buried Jennifer’s parents without knowing whether Jennifer was still alive.
  • A new search warrant revealed a .22-caliber pistol was removed from the Short home, in addition to other weapons.

August 23, 2002

  • Strong Lead Fails to Develop: Investigators received what Sheriff Frank Cassell characterized as a strong lead. Detectives pursued the information.
  • It ultimately failed to produce the hoped-for breakthrough. Source

August 25, 2002

  • Ten days after Jennifer disappeared, some investigators received their first meaningful break from the investigation after working extremely long hours.
  • Sheriff Cassell said at least four investigators would remain assigned to the case. The strong lead received Friday night had not panned out.
  • Authorities continued expressing hope that Jennifer might be found alive.
  • DNA testing had also been conducted concerning whether Michael and Mary were Jennifer’s biological parents. Cassell said they had received the results but declined to release them publicly.
  • Authorities said they still had no suspect. The reward had reached approximately $11,000.

August 26, 2022

  • Police received information concerning a possible stalker of Mary Short from when she worked at Pluma in 1992
  • First Investigation of Garrison Bowman: Law enforcement received information concerning Garrison Bowman. Source
    • Bowman’s landlord reportedly told authorities that Bowman had made statements about wanting or threatening to kill a Virginia mobile-home mover following a dispute involving the movement of a mobile home. Michael Short operated a mobile-home moving company.
  • Investigators searched property associated with Bowman. The FBI interviewed Bowman during August.
  • At this stage, investigators did not publicly establish that Bowman and Michael Short had actually been involved in the dispute described by the informant. That distinction later became important.

September 4, 2002

  • Police exhumed Michael Short’s body for additional forensic testing.

September 5, 2002

  • Postal officials initially denied investigators access to the Shorts’ mail. The issue was subsequently resolved. By then the reward exceeded $50,000.

September 9, 2002

  • Authorities obtained search warrants connected to Bowman. Source
    • The warrants were based partly on statements from Rick Lemons concerning Bowman’s alleged threats and activities shortly before the murders.
    • Investigators searched Bowman’s former residence and collected evidence for testing.

September 15, 2002

  • Police scaled down physical searches. Police said they would conduct no additional land, helicopter, or dive searches unless new information justified them.

September 19, 2002

  • Police released 10-year-old photographs of Mary Short hoping to generate information about the alleged Pluma “stalker” incident.

September 20, 2002

  • Jennifer’s aunt and uncle, Ruby and Chris Young, were named her legal guardians if she was found.

September 24, 2002

  • Bowman’s landlord returned to the Short residence and spoke with investigators. Source

September 25, 2002

  • 1:30 p.m. Human Remains Discovered: A small human skull with hair still attached was discovered at the property of Eddie Albert on Grogan Road in Stoneville, Rockingham County, North Carolina, approximately 30 minutes south of Oak Level, Virginia. Source
    • Albert’s daughter, Lisa Albert, told The Roanoke Times that the family’s dogs had been seen tossing around what initially looked like a brown wig in the driveway.
    • After authorities arrived, investigators discovered teeth and bone fragments scattered around the yard.
    • A subsequent foot search of the surrounding property located a section of ribs in a shallow stream bed and a cluster of bone fragments in a small pond behind the residence.
    • Detectives began dragging the pond to determine whether additional remains were underwater.
    • Cadaver dogs from the Triad Bloodhound Team in Forsyth County, North Carolina, were brought in to search the surrounding woods. The dogs found no additional bones that day.
  • Later: The search was suspended at sunset and scheduled to resume the following morning. Source
  • The remains were scheduled to be transported to the North Carolina state medical examiner’s office in Chapel Hill for examination.
    • Henry County investigators obtained a hair sample from the skull and other evidence and transported the material to Roanoke, where Jennifer’s belongings were being held, in an attempt to make a preliminary comparison.
    • At that point, neither Virginia nor North Carolina authorities would publicly identify the remains as Jennifer’s. Rockingham County Sheriff Sam Page said he could neither confirm nor rule it out, while Henry County Sheriff Frank Cassell said he was doubtful but acknowledged they did not yet know.
    • Investigators also checked a Civil War-era cemetery near the Albert property to determine whether any graves had been disturbed. Deputies found no evidence of disturbed graves.
    • Authorities planned to compare the discovery against missing-person cases within a 50-mile radius.
    • An unusual detail Sheriff Cassell noted was that the Henry County Sheriff’s Office initially learned about the discovery from a North Carolina news organization rather than North Carolina law enforcement. Cassell said this made him suspicious, although a Rockingham County Sheriff’s Office spokesperson suggested local authorities may simply have wanted to determine what they were dealing with before contacting Virginia investigators.
  • Connection to Bowman Property: The location of Jennifer’s remains attracted renewed attention to Garrison Bowman. Source
    • Contemporary reporting stated that Jennifer’s remains were discovered approximately one-half mile to one mile from a mobile home Bowman had occupied or owned before leaving for Canada. Different contemporary accounts gave slightly different distance estimates.
    • This proximity led investigators to take a second, closer look at Bowman.

September 26, 2002

  • The remains were described as those of a 9-to-10-year-old Caucasian female who had been shot in the head.
  • Recovered remains included portions of the skull, rib cage, teeth, jaw, and hair.

September 28, 2002

  • Authorities searched Bowman’s mobile home, collecting additional evidence. Source
  • Bowman remained outside the continental United States while investigators attempted to determine whether he had any connection to the Short family killings.

September 30, 2002

  • Forensic Examination: Preliminary examination indicated the remains belonged to a young female, increasing concern that they could be Jennifer. Dental analysis indicated the remains belonged to a 9-year-old white female. The skull showed evidence of a gunshot wound to the head, consistent with the manner in which Michael and Mary Short had been killed. Investigators stressed that this did not yet constitute a positive identification of Jennifer. Source
    • Investigators transported bone fragments to Roanoke for DNA analysis.
    • The North Carolina medical examiner determined that the child represented by the remains had been shot once in the head.
    • Investigators awaited DNA confirmation.

October 1, 2002

  • Search of Pond: Divers searched a pond near the Grogan Road recovery site for additional evidence. Source
    • Approximately 20 reporters watched as a line of searchers walked through marshland and investigators used poking sticks in the grass.
    • Divers searched for approximately five hours.
    • They located bones in the water, but the remains proved to be from an animal.
    • Authorities continued searching for additional portions of the child’s remains and other evidence potentially connected to the homicide.
  • Family Member Accepts Remains Are Likely Jennifer’s: James Whitehead, husband of Jennifer’s aunt Carol Whitehead, told The Roanoke Times that he had accepted that the remains were probably Jennifer’s even before DNA confirmation. Source
    • Whitehead said the family had hoped Jennifer would be found alive but that there were “too many coincidences” for him to believe the remains belonged to another child.
    • The report noted that authorities had previously determined the remains belonged to a Caucasian girl approximately 9 years old with brown hair, and that there were no other known missing children fitting that description in the immediate region.

October 2, 2002

  • Initial DNA testing had not yet produced a conclusive identification, and additional testing continued.

October 3, 2002

  • Garrison “Gary” Bowman was arrested by the Royal Canadian Mounted Police in Inuvik, Northwest Territories, Canada, after an incident involving alleged impaired driving. Canadian authorities subsequently held Bowman on an immigration violation. Source
    • Bowman had left North Carolina shortly after the Short family murders and had been living in the Northwest Territories.
  • According to a later U.S. Department of Justice Office of the Inspector General report, law enforcement investigating Jennifer Short’s murder learned that Bowman was in Canadian custody after a name check was run through the National Crime Information Center (NCIC). Source
    • The NCIC inquiry revealed that the Royal Canadian Mounted Police had arrested the individual investigators wanted to question in connection with the Jennifer Short investigation.
    • After learning Bowman was in custody, investigators began making arrangements to travel to Canada to interview him and search his van.
    • The DOJ Inspector General later reported that local investigators initially appeared to believe that a North Carolina search warrant could be used to search Bowman’s vehicle in Canada. When the FBI Legal Attaché in Canada learned of the situation, the Legal Attaché attempted to coordinate with the local FBI office and investigators because a North Carolina warrant could not simply be executed in Canada. Source
    • Canadian authorities subsequently transferred Bowman from Inuvik to Yellowknife, where he remained in custody while immigration proceedings were conducted.
  • Late Night: The Virginia Division of Forensic Science laboratory in Roanoke obtained DNA results positively matching the remains to Jennifer Renee Short.

October 4, 2002

  • Jennifer Identified: Rockingham County Sheriff Sam Page announced that DNA testing conducted by the Virginia Division of Forensic Science laboratory in Roanoke positively identified the skeletal remains recovered in Rockingham County on September 25 as Jennifer Renee Short. Investigators had received the positive DNA results late the previous night. The medical examiner determined that Jennifer had suffered a gunshot wound to the head.
    • Sheriff Cassell said investigators had cleared up questions concerning Jennifer’s parentage. Source
    • Police said there was no doubt Michael Short was Jennifer’s biological father.
    • Cassell also stated there was no evidence that either Michael or Mary Short was anything other than Jennifer’s biological parent.
    • Authorities said the clarification was meant to eliminate speculation and let investigators focus on legitimate leads.
  • Authorities publicly confirmed that Bowman was in Canadian custody. Rockingham County Sheriff Sam Page emphasized that Bowman had not been identified as a suspect, but investigators considered him an important person they needed to interview. Source
  • Bowman’s location had become increasingly significant after Jennifer Short’s remains were discovered in Rockingham County near property associated with him, and investigators recovered evidence during searches of his North Carolina property.

October 5, 2002

  • Investigators announced plans to travel to Canada to question Garrison S. Bowman, 60, of Mayodan, North Carolina. Source
    • Jennifer’s remains were recovered approximately 1.5 miles from Bowman’s trailer.
    • Canadian authorities were holding Bowman. He had been encountered in Canada in connection with unrelated matters, including allegations concerning driving while intoxicated and immigration issues.
    • Investigators in Virginia and North Carolina wanted to question him concerning the Short case. They also sought access to Bowman’s vehicle.
    • Bowman had not been charged with the Short murders.

October 7, 2002

  • Bowman appeared at a Canadian detention hearing in Yellowknife. Source
    • During Canadian proceedings, authorities described information alleging that Bowman had been involved in a business dispute connected to moving a mobile home.
    • A Canadian judge ordered Bowman held for an additional seven days.
    • The court reportedly considered him a flight risk and someone who might pose a danger to the public.
  • Investigators planned to travel to Yellowknife, Northwest Territories, Canada, to interview Garrison Bowman and search his van after learning Canadian authorities held him. Source
    • Police disclosed that they had known about Bowman earlier but became more interested after Jennifer’s remains were discovered near his home.
    • Police described him as a possible witness, not a suspect.
    • Their objectives included interviewing Bowman and searching his van.

October 8, 2002

  • Plans to interview Bowman were temporarily delayed while investigators waited for the diplomatic and legal authorization necessary to conduct investigative activity in Canada. The delay concerned how American investigators could legally question Bowman and obtain evidence located inside Canada, not whether investigators intended to travel there. Source
  • Sheriff Cassell said investigators wanted to learn everything they could about Bowman.
  • Cassell explained that once Jennifer’s remains were discovered near Bowman’s former trailer, authorities realized they needed to examine him again.
  • Bowman publicly complained that false information had been provided to the FBI and North Carolina authorities.
  • Investigators continued stressing publicly that Bowman had not been charged with killing the Short family.

October 10, 2002

  • Four investigators from the Henry County Sheriff’s Office and Rockingham County Sheriff’s Office traveled to Yellowknife, Northwest Territories, Canada, as part of the investigation into the murders of Michael, Mary and Jennifer Short. Investigators sought to question Garrison Bowman and pursue evidence associated with his van. Source
  • The FBI Legal Attaché became involved after learning that local investigators intended to travel to Canada to interview Bowman and search his van, apparently believing that a North Carolina search warrant could be used in Canada. The Legal Attaché attempted to intervene because the search required coordination with Canadian authorities and compliance with Canadian law. Source

October 11, 2002

  • Investigators were in Yellowknife. Source
  • Two Rockingham County investigators interviewed Bowman following his Canadian deportation hearing.
  • The two Henry County investigators did not participate in that interview. Sheriff Frank Cassell said his investigators planned to question Bowman after he returned to the United States.
  • Canadian authorities continued processing Bowman for deportation.

October 12, 2002

  • Jennifer’s Funeral: After DNA identification, authorities made arrangements to return Jennifer’s remains to her family. Source
    • The Martinsville Church of God planned visitation and funeral services.
    • Jennifer was buried after approximately six weeks of uncertainty concerning her fate.
  • Canadian authorities scheduled Bowman for deportation over immigration violations. Investigators postponed an interview until his return to the United States. Jennifer’s funeral was held. Source

October 14, 2002

  • The investigators returned from Canada. Source
  • Authorities said Bowman was expected to be deported to the United States within approximately two weeks.
  • Investigators planned to question him further after his return.
  • The investigation into his van remained unresolved because the vehicle was still in Canada.

October 21, 2002

  • Bowman remained in Yellowknife awaiting deportation. Source
  • Investigators from the FBI, Virginia and North Carolina planned to meet him after he was returned to the United States.
  • Investigators also planned another trip to Inuvik to obtain access to and search Bowman’s van through the appropriate Canadian procedures.

October 22, 2002

  • Bowman continued stating that he would cooperate with investigators.

October 23, 2002

  • Bowman was ordered detained so a grand jury could question him.

October 29, 2002

  • Investigators returned to Canada to inspect Bowman’s van for forensic evidence.

October 30, 2002

  • A federal magistrate dismissed the material-witness warrant and released Bowman from custody after federal prosecutors said he had cooperated with investigators and there was no longer a reason to detain him. He was required to appear before a federal grand jury on November 12, 2002, to answer additional questions concerning the Short investigation. Source
    • Garrison “Gary” Bowman, a retired carpenter from Mayodan, North Carolina, was released from the Roanoke City Jail after a federal magistrate determined there was no longer a basis to keep him detained as a material witness. Source
    • Bowman had been returned from Canada and detained on a federal material-witness warrant connected to the Short investigation.

  • Virginia Adopts Statewide AMBER Alert Following Jennifer Short’s Abduction: Approximately three months after 9-year-old Jennifer Short disappeared from her Henry County home, Virginia adopted a statewide AMBER Alert plan designed to rapidly distribute information about abducted children. Source
  • The Roanoke Times specifically reported that the program was adopted after Jennifer’s disappearance and other similar tragedies.
  • Under the new system, Virginia State Police would operate the AMBER Alert Plan and coordinate efforts statewide.
  • When activated, police could quickly distribute information through radio and television broadcasts.

March 4, 2005

  • U.S. Attorney John Brownlee announced that three men faced federal charges for lying to investigators about the Short case. Source
  • False Information and Perjury Charges: Federal prosecutors announced charges stemming from false information provided during the Short family murder investigation. Timothy Fennon Sampson and Jerry Riley Mills were charged in an eight-count federal indictment with offenses including conspiracy, perjury and providing false information to investigators. Source
    • Sampson had first contacted the Rockingham County Sheriff’s Office on August 15, 2003, exactly one year after the murders, claiming that he had witnessed the crimes. Source
    • Sampson claimed that he had been near the Short residence during the early morning hours of August 15, 2002, heard gunshots, and heard a child saying “No, no.”
    • He claimed that he saw a man emerge from the Short residence with a weapon and then return outside carrying a child.
    • Sampson later testified before a federal grand jury that the man resembled Abraham Lincoln and eventually connected the name Bowman to the person he claimed to have seen. This was significant because investigators had already identified Garrison Bowman as a person of interest, and Bowman had been described as having a beard and resembling Lincoln.
    • Sampson subsequently recruited Jerry Riley Mills to support his fabricated account. According to the federal court record, Sampson drove Mills to Henry County, showed him the Short murder scene, and rehearsed the false story with him before Mills was interviewed by investigators.
    • Mills later provided false testimony to the federal grand jury. At Sampson’s subsequent trial, Mills admitted his grand jury testimony was untrue.
    • Sampson also recruited his daughter, Beth Braxton, to provide false corroborating testimony. She initially told the grand jury that Sampson had returned home on the morning of the murders crying and shaking and saying that he had witnessed someone being killed. Braxton later recanted that testimony and testified that she had said what Sampson instructed her to say.
    • Sampson also attempted to recruit Michael Holland to falsely tell FBI investigators that he had sold Mills a Ford van and later had the vehicle crushed. Holland initially repeated the story but quickly contacted investigators and admitted that he had been instructed to lie.
    • Sampson subsequently made threatening telephone calls to investigators, including Rockingham County Sheriff’s Capt. Bobby Lawson and Henry County Sheriff’s Maj. Kimmi Nester. The threats later contributed to a federal sentencing enhancement for obstruction of justice. Source
    • Investigators testified that Sampson’s fabricated claims caused law enforcement to expend as many as 1,000 additional investigative hours pursuing false information in the Short case. Source
  • Tony Lee Epperson Charged: Federal prosecutors separately charged Tony Lee Epperson with providing false information during the Short investigation.
    • Epperson had approached investigators with information concerning the homicides.
    • U.S. Attorney John L. Brownlee personally participated in an interview with Epperson that lasted about five or six hours. Prosecutors ultimately concluded that Epperson’s account was false.
    • According to Brownlee, Epperson subsequently acknowledged that the information he provided was false.

Evidence Removed From the Short Family Home: 

Investigators conducted an extensive search of the Short family residence at 10680 Virginia Avenue in Henry County, Virginia, following the discovery of Michael and Mary Short on August 15, 2002. Dozens of items were removed from the residence for examination as investigators worked to reconstruct the murders and determine what happened to 9-year-old Jennifer Short. Source

Crime Scene and Ballistics Evidence

  • Investigators recovered two .22-caliber cartridge casings from separate areas of the residence. Investigators recovered one casing from Mary Short’s bed and the second from the garage floor near Michael Short.
  • Gunshot residue testing was performed on both Michael and Mary Short.
  • Investigators collected bedding associated with both victims, including bed covers and a bed sheet from Mary’s bed, several of Mary’s pillows, and blankets located beneath Michael in the garage.
  • A swab of a red-colored stain from the wall near Mary’s bed was collected for forensic examination.
  • Several firearms and ammunition were removed from the residence, including a Marlin Model 60 .22-caliber firearm, a Winchester Model 1500XTR 12-gauge shotgun, .22-caliber ammunition, and 12-gauge shotgun shells.
  • The recovery of a .22-caliber firearm from the residence does not establish that it was the weapon used in the murders. Investigators could compare the firearm and ammunition with the ballistic evidence recovered from the crime scene.

Jennifer Short’s Bedroom

  • Jennifer’s bedroom was extensively processed for evidence after investigators discovered that she was missing.
  • Investigators removed a pillow found on the floor of Jennifer’s bedroom, another pillow from her bed, and a flashlight found on her bed.
  • Investigators collected a multicolored blanket, two baby blankets, three additional blankets, two mattress covers and a bed skirt from Jennifer’s bed.
  • Investigators removed multiple pieces of Jennifer’s clothing and shoes.
  • Investigators collected three hairbrushes from Jennifer’s dresser, which may provide known hair or biological samples for comparison.
  • Investigators also collected multiple toothbrushes from the bathroom.
  • Investigators seized pages containing names and telephone numbers from Jennifer’s room.
  • The location of the pillow and flashlight are noteworthy crime-scene details, but the publicly available evidence inventory does not establish whether investigators determined that either item was connected to Jennifer’s abduction.

Garage Evidence

  • Investigators collected the blankets located beneath Michael Short’s body in the garage.
  • Investigators collected a shirt, shorts, a watch, and a lighter from a chair beside Michael.
  • Investigators documented an impression on the garage-door window glass containing the words “I’m glad to see.”
  • Publicly available information does not establish who wrote the words, when they were written or whether investigators ultimately determined that the impression was connected to the murders.

Cut Telephone Line and Communications Evidence

  • Investigators collected telephone wire from the south end of the residence at the telephone box.
  • The telephone line serving the Short residence had been interrupted around the time of the murders. Preserving the wire allowed investigators to examine where and how it was cut.
  • Investigators also collected telephone-related records, including a Sprint residential telephone bill and cellular telephone billing records.
  • Investigators recovered an Audiovox cellular telephone from a shed, where it was reportedly located on a lawnmower.
  • The physical telephone wire and telephone records potentially allowed investigators to reconstruct communications to and from the residence before the murders.

Reconstructing the Family’s Final Hours

  • Several items recovered from the residence could help investigators reconstruct Michael, Mary and Jennifer’s activities during their final evening.
  • Investigators recovered a Walmart receipt dated August 14, 2002, from the kitchen counter.
  • Investigators recovered a Burger King receipt timestamped 11:05 p.m. from the kitchen trash can.
  • The receipts provided investigators with potential transaction points during the family’s final evening. The publicly released evidence inventory itself does not establish which member of the Short family made either purchase.
  • Other items collected from the kitchen included a 16-ounce Mountain Dew bottle, a 10-ounce bottle of NyQuil, a green Tupperware cup, an ashtray and its contents, a clear bottle containing brown fluid, medications belonging to Michael and a paper note.
  • Investigators also collected the contents of the kitchen trash can, potentially preserving discarded material from immediately before the murders.
  • These items could potentially provide fingerprints, DNA, trace evidence or other information about people who had been inside the residence.

Cash and Personal Property

  • Cash and other valuables remained inside the Short residence following the murders.
  • Michael’s brown bifold wallet contained approximately $75.
  • Mary’s purse contained approximately $45 in cash and two blank checks, along with identification and other documents.
  • Investigators also recovered approximately $485 in cash and a $211.41 check associated with Bank of Ferrum material.
  • Investigators collected a Bank of Ferrum money bag containing documents from the kitchen counter.
  • Investigators recovered three photographs of Jennifer from Mary’s purse.
  • Investigators recovered a key ring containing multiple keys from the kitchen counter.
  • Investigators also collected a set of keys associated with a Toyota pickup truck at the residence.
  • The presence of cash, checks, and other valuables inside the residence became relevant as investigators considered possible motives for the murders, including whether robbery was a likely motive.

Michael Short’s Business Records

  • Investigators removed extensive records associated with Michael’s mobile-home moving business.
  • Evidence collected included a payroll book, business checkbook, telephone bills, business documents, a computer disk, mail and an aluminum-colored briefcase containing additional documents.
  • Investigators also collected a box containing business documents, computer material and mail from the office.
  • Investigators seized numerous documents containing names and telephone numbers from different areas of the residence, including the kitchen and Jennifer’s room.
  • The records provided investigators with potential customers, employees, business associates and other people who had contact with Michael before the murders.
  • Michael’s business, M.S. Mobile Home Movers, has remained relevant to the investigation. Authorities have continued asking anyone who had a mobile home moved by Michael during 2002 and was never interviewed to contact investigators.

Photographs, Film and Reference Samples

  • Investigators collected multiple rolls of unprocessed film from an office desk.
  • Investigators recovered three photographs of Jennifer from Mary’s purse.
  • Investigators collected a carpet sample from the residence.
  • Investigators recovered three hairbrushes from Jennifer’s bedroom.
  • Investigators collected multiple toothbrushes from the bathroom.
  • Investigators recovered a “Baby’s Milestones” book containing hair fiber and footprints from beneath Mary Short’s bed.
  • A “Baby’s Health and Growth Record” was also recovered from beneath Mary’s bed.
  • These items could potentially provide known comparison samples for forensic examination, although publicly available information does not establish precisely how each item was subsequently used.

Additional Evidence Collected

  • Investigators collected a notebook from the nightstand in Mary Short’s bedroom.
  • Investigators seized mail delivered to the residence, including cellular and residential telephone billing records.
  • Investigators collected multiple sets of keys and numerous documents containing names and telephone numbers.
  • Investigators also removed what the contemporary evidence inventory described as a central air processor unit, ACS brand, associated with Mary Short.
  • Investigators ultimately removed dozens of individual pieces of physical, documentary, and potential biological evidence from the Short residence, preserving material that could potentially be reexamined as forensic technology improved.

Forensic Reexamination of Evidence

  • The Short family investigation generated a substantial collection of physical evidence in 2002. Some of that evidence underwent forensic testing during the original investigation and was preserved for potential future examination.
  • In 2021, investigators announced that evidence from the Short family murders was being reevaluated to determine whether advances in forensic science and DNA technology could provide information that was unavailable to investigators in 2002.
  • The preservation of the original crime-scene evidence is particularly significant in a decades-old unsolved homicide because modern DNA testing can sometimes obtain useful profiles from evidence that previously produced limited or inconclusive results. Source

Garrison Bowman

Investigative Subject

Garrison “Gary” Bowman

Case context: Bowman was extensively investigated, but he was never charged with murdering Michael, Mary, or Jennifer Short. The information below describes the investigative history and should not be read as identifying him as the killer.

Why Investigators Became Interested

  • Bowman was a carpenter living in Rockingham County.
  • Investigators received information that he had threatened a Virginia mobile-home mover during a dispute involving a mobile home.
  • Michael operated a mobile-home moving business.
  • Investigators searched Bowman’s rental property during August 2002. The FBI interviewed him.
  • Bowman departed North Carolina on August 16 and eventually traveled to Canada.

Why Interest Intensified

  • Jennifer’s remains were later discovered close to a mobile home associated with Bowman.
  • Investigators searched Bowman’s property again. Nine maps were seized.
  • One map reportedly contained a marking showing or near the Short family’s location. Fox News
  • These circumstances made Bowman an important subject of the investigation. They did not prove he committed the murders.

Canada

  • Bowman was detained in Canada for unrelated immigration and alcohol-related matters.
  • Virginia and North Carolina investigators traveled to Canada seeking to interview him and examine his van.
  • He was ultimately returned to the United States.

Material-Witness Warrant

  • Federal authorities detained Bowman as a material witness. He was extensively questioned.
  • The warrant was later dismissed with prosecutors noting that Bowman had returned and was cooperating.
  • He was released October 30, 2002.

Grand Jury

  • Bowman later appeared before a federal grand jury investigating the case.
  • Authorities continued referring to him as a witness or person of interest.
  • No indictment charging him with the Short murders resulted.

False Information

  • The case against Bowman was further complicated when information supplied by people seeking reward money was determined to be fabricated.
  • Multiple individuals were eventually convicted of false information or related offenses stemming from the Short investigation.

Investigative Outcome

  • Bowman was never charged with murdering Michael, Mary, or Jennifer.
  • By 2007, law enforcement publicly acknowledged that the evidence had not developed into a prosecutable homicide case against him.
  • Any timeline should therefore describe Bowman as a person extensively investigated, not as the killer.

Modern Forensic Review

The investigative focus is broader than any single historical person of interest. Authorities have repeatedly revisited preserved evidence, old interviews, business contacts, South Carolina leads, and the complete case file.

  • Beginning in 2021, a multi-agency task force examined preserved physical evidence for new forensic opportunities.
  • In 2023, another fresh-eyes task force received the entire case file for renewed review.
  • By 2025, evidence collected in 2002 had been submitted to DNA Labs International for advanced DNA testing, funded by the FBI.
  • Authorities have not publicly disclosed whether they have developed a complete unknown DNA profile or whether they are using forensic genetic genealogy.

Theories

Investigative Theories

Major Investigative Theories

Jennifer Was the Target

  • One possibility is that Jennifer was the intended victim.
  • Under this theory, Michael and Mary may have been killed so the offender could abduct Jennifer without interference.
  • Jennifer’s removal from the residence supports consideration of this theory.
  • No publicly released evidence currently proves it.

Michael Was the Target

  • Another possibility is that Michael was targeted because of a personal or business dispute.
  • The investigation into Michael’s mobile-home business, customers, and South Carolina activities demonstrates that law enforcement has considered his relationships an important avenue.
  • Under this theory, Mary could have been killed to eliminate a witness, and Jennifer could have been abducted because she saw or recognized the offender.
  • Again, no publicly available evidence establishes this as the motive.

Entire Family Was Targeted

  • The deliberate cutting of the telephone line and the killings of all three family members raise the possibility that the offender intended to eliminate the entire Short household.
  • This theory does not explain why Jennifer was transported away from the home.

One Offender or Multiple Offenders

  • Authorities have never publicly established whether one person or multiple people committed the crime.
  • A single offender could theoretically have carried out the shootings and removed Jennifer.
  • Multiple offenders could explain the ability to control three victims and transport Jennifer.
  • Neither possibility has been established publicly.

What the Killer Appears to Have Known

Certain facts allow investigators to examine whether the offender knew the family.

  • The offender apparently had enough opportunity to approach the Short residence overnight or in the early morning.
  • The telephone line was cut. Michael was found in his garage. Mary was in her bedroom. Authorities successfully removed Jennifer from the residence. Her remains were deposited in rural North Carolina.

These circumstances naturally raise questions about whether the killer knew:

  • The Short family’s routines
  • The property’s layout
  • Michael’s sleeping or television habits
  • Jennifer’s bedroom location
  • The surrounding roads
  • The rural Rockingham County disposal area

These remain investigative questions, not established conclusions.

The Rick Lemons/Bowman account was essentially:

Question: Were the allegations later associated with Timothy Fennon Sampson connected in any way to information originally provided by Rick Lemons about Garry Storm Bowman?

  • Rick Lemons told investigators that Bowman had complained about a Virginia man who allegedly owed him money for work and had made a statement about having to kill the man. Lemons also reported that, on or around the day the Short family was discovered, Bowman discussed killing a Virginia businessman who had not paid him for trailer-related work.
  • Years later, Timothy Fennon Sampson became involved in the Short investigation after providing information to authorities that ultimately led to federal charges for false statements and testimony.

The similarity raises several unanswered questions:

  • Did Sampson independently provide investigators with a story resembling the information attributed to Bowman by Lemons?
  • Did Sampson know Rick Lemons, Gary Lemons, Garry Bowman, or anyone within their circle?
  • Could Sampson have learned details of the Bowman allegations through another person, investigators, or media coverage before giving his account?
  • Were investigators dealing with two genuinely independent sources, or did portions of the information originate from the same underlying story?
  • Did investigators ever examine whether there was a connection between Sampson and the people responsible for the original Bowman lead?

No evidence currently establishes that Sampson and Lemons were related or personally connected. The similarity between the accounts should therefore be treated as an investigative question rather than evidence of a relationship or conspiracy.
