Meanwhile, the reward

has dwindled from more than
$60,000 to roughly $10,000,
as several contributors have
pulled out for various rea-
sons, said Lt. Kimmy Nester

Gwaltney said the FBI
hopes to add to the reward
and also create a Web site

would allow people to leave
tips.

The Short family killings
sent tremors through the
Henry County community
five years ago. Then-Sher-
iff Frank Cassell became a

familiar presence in front of

TV cameras, often reassuring
residents that he would pur-
sue the case relentlessly.

But it was Cassell who
would deliver another jolt to
those he was sworn to serve

and protect.

In November, the sheriff
and a dozen deputies were
indicted in a sweeping fed-
eral corruption probe that
centered on allegations of
drug-dealing by sheriff’s
office employees. A total of 20
people were indicted. Cassell
and 16 of the defendants have
pleaded guilty.

['he corruption indictments
led to speculation that shady
activities within the sheriff’s
office might have hindered
the Short investigation, or
even that the two cases were
linked.

But Nester and FBI officials
say there is no known con-
nection, adding that the Short
investigation was not seri-
ously impeded by problems at
the sheriff’s office.

“We're aware that some
people ... question is there
any connection,” Nester said.
“We put our armor on and
keep trucking.”

Gwaltney said that none of
the 20 defendants in the cor
ruption case has been ques-
tioned or was under suspicion
in the Short family killings.

He added, though, that the
investigation into the sheriff’s
office created some awkward
moments for members of the
task force investigating the
Short case.

“Last week, you were work-
ing,, sidn by side with an offi-
cer,” he said, “and the follow-
ing week ... you're indicting
some of his co-workers.”

A staffing shortage created
by the indictments had anoth-
er effect, Nester said. One of
two Henry County investiga-
tors who were working full
time on the Short case had to
assume other duties.

The task force is made up
of about 10 investigators from
the FBI, the Bureau of Alcohol
Tobacco Firearms and Explo-
sives, the Virginia State Police
and the sheriff’s departments
in Henry County and Rocking-
ham County, N.C.

FBI officials described the
task force as very active, with
some investigators devoted
almost entirely to the Short
case. The task force meets
weekly.

As the detectives chase new
leads and pore over mounds of
evidence, relatives of Michael,
Mary and Jennifer Short con-
tinue to wait for a resolution.

“Every family member
would like to see this thing
solved,” said Michael Short’s
uncle Frank Arrington, 72, of
Moneta. “I really don’t under-
stand how someone could go
in that home and murder an
entire family and not leave a
trace.”
