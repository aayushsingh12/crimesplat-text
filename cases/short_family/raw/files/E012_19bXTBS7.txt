News about
Jennifer Short
saddens us all

The TV was on and the news was
bad. Forensic evidence showed that
skeletal remains found in Rockingham
County, N.C., were those of Jennifer
Renee Short, the Virginia girl who dis-
appeared Aug. 15.

Her parents, Michael and Mary
Short, each suffered a fatal bullet
wound to the head that day.

It took no genius to expect this
news, yet when
authorities made it
official, it felt like a
cannonball to the
gut. Hope lives on
when kids are
involved. Now, we
can only pray that
9-year-old Jennifer
didn’t suffer much.

You'd have to
g0 back quite a
ways to find a
crime that so mobi-
lized Southwest Virginia's finer
instincts.

Reward funds sprang up to encour-
age the sharing of any tidbit of news
about the case. Posters appeared not
just in Henry County but also in
Roanoke and other areas.

People prayed for Jennifer, but
more than that, they pulled for her. Her
end is hardest, no doubt, for her imme-
diate family to accept. It's little easier
for her extended family of millions who
have followed the case from its shock-
ing start.

CUPPA JOE

JOE KENNEDY

One step at a time

Friday’s news conference let us
know that Garry Storm Bowman, a wit-
ness but not a suspect, is out of the
country. Authorities said they know
where he is and that they will interview
him.

The reclusive carpenter hasn't been
seen around his North Carolina home
since Aug. 16, the day after Jennifer's
parents were found shot to death in Oak
Level in Henry County. He reportedly
told a neighbor that a Virginia man
owed him money.

Henry County Sheriff Frank Cassell
has repeatedly urged the media and
public not to make too much of this sin-
gle witness, but some in the community
feel a strong desire to blame him. The
Rev. Jackie Poe, who officiated at
Michael and Mary Short’s funeral, said
he felt it, too.

We must resist it, and let the inves-
tigators do their work. If the case is set-
tled, it will be settled in a courtroom,
not at a news conference, in a barroom
or over our dining room tables.

Poe, pastor of the Martinsville
Church of God, rushed home Friday to
flip on his TV and catch the news con-
ference. Jennifer’s aunt and uncle Ruby
and Chris Young belong to his congre-
gation. Weeks earlier, Poe said he was
convinced that prayer would keep Jen-
nifer safe and alive. It didn’t work out
that way.

“I really feel totally let down and
disappointed,” he said. “I feel the pain
that I feel the whole community feels —
not only the community but . . . the
nation. . . .Everybody followed this
story closely.”

Acceptance and some relief

As soon as he heard the news, Poe
called the Youngs “and let them know
that when they need me, just let me
know.” Otherwise, he would honor their
privacy.

He praised Cassell for explicitly
stating that Jennifer was the biological
child of Michael and Mary Short, and
that no evidence indicated that they
were anything other than upright,
decent people. Cassell apologized for
circumstances that kept him from say-
ing it sooner.

But the minister doubted whether
Friday's news would provide the “clo-
sure” everyone talks about. Acceptance,
eventually, perhaps. But not closure.

The wheels of justice grind slowly,
but they do grind. At times it seemed
that Friday's news would never come. It
was not the news we wanted, or the
answer to our prayers.

But as Cassell noted, it did give us
one small piece of relief. Jennifer Renee
Short is safe from further harm.

“That brings a sense of peace to
me,” Poe said, “knowing she is with the
Lord.”
